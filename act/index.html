Two minutes, one call

Tell Congress: AI companies should report when things go wrong.

Right now, there's no national rule that makes AI companies tell anyone when their AI is misused or something goes badly wrong. We mostly find out when a company decides to tell us. Nuclear power plants have to report problems. AI companies should too.

Here from an earlier episode? Its action is still below.

Find your members of Congress

We don't collect your ZIP code. The lookup happens in your browser.

Never called Congress? It's a one-minute call.

A staffer answers, asks your name and ZIP code, and writes down your view. Nobody debates you. Your script is right under each name.

More about what happens when you call
  • A staffer or intern answers. They're not the member of Congress, and they're not going to argue with you.
  • They'll ask your name and ZIP code, to confirm you're a constituent.
  • You read the script. They note your topic and which side you're on. That's it.
  • Got voicemail? Leave the same message. It still gets counted.
  • You don't need to be an expert. "I'm a constituent and I care about this" is the whole qualification.

Call (best) or write

Writing instead? “Copy message & write” copies a short message and opens their contact form. Paste it in. Their forms ask for your address to confirm you're a constituent.
